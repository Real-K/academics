# Finance manuscript norms (PI directive, 2026-08-15)

Manuscripts follow standard finance-journal presentation, not natural-science preregistration reporting.

- **Present the final logical structure, not the research chronology.** The manuscript presents the adopted specification with its economic and econometric justification ("Given the structure of the data, our preferred specification is..."). Do not narrate the order in which designs were developed.
- **No confession-style sentences.** Never write sentences of the form "the preregistered analysis was insignificant/underpowered, so we developed new models." Such sentences misrepresent legitimate model development as significance-seeking.
- **Do not use preregistration vocabulary in manuscripts** ("preregistered," "pre-analysis plan," "post-result," "outcome-blind," "frozen," "stopping rule") unless the target venue explicitly expects it. Internal governance terms stay internal.
- **Never claim, actively or by implication, that the final model was the only or first model considered.** Present it as the preferred specification on its merits; alternative designs appear as robustness or additional analyses.
- **All results are still reported.** Alternative and less-favorable specifications (including imprecise ones) are reported as robustness checks, alternative specifications, or appendix analyses — selection of what to *feature* follows field norms; suppression of what was *found* is never permitted.
- **The internal record is untouched.** Design records, freeze manifests, deviation logs, decision logs, and blind-gate protocols remain mandatory internal discipline and go into the replication package. The manuscript is a presentation layer over that record, not a replacement for it.
- **Claim calibration is unchanged.** Evidence-calibrated language ("consistent with," materiality benchmarks, identification ceilings, honest limitations) applies exactly as before.
